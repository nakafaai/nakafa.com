# ADR 0047: The Island Is a Game

## Status

Accepted on 9 November 2026. It replaces the learner-facing half of ADR 0042 (the renderer, build mode, the home frame and sharing, D12 and D14 to D22), which the owner withdrew on 9 November. ADR 0042's data decisions (the tables, the Stars ledger, the grants, the level table) still hold, except where D6 below changes what the island row stores.

## Context

The owner rejected the first island on 9 November: it drew small, still, inside the lessons' `VisualCard`, on flat grey bands, under four cards that only listed how many Stars a place earns. In the owner's words the island should be a game: building blocks like Minecraft, a pixel art style like Stardew Valley, its own UI and its own art, a world that fills the screen, and nothing static: the learner drags, pans, zooms, clicks and plays. Stars from lessons and try-outs unlock its blocks and items.

### What the references do and where they fall short

| Reference | Studied | Takes from it | Falls short on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minecraft Classic in the browser (classic.minecraft.net) | its page through REA on 9 November: one 8.4 MB `app.js` on Babylon.js, seven helper canvases, block previews and the hotbar as PNG files | a hotbar of nine blocks, blocks placed on the face a ray hits, removal by the same ray | 8.4 MB before the first frame; pointer-lock first person, which a phone cannot play; no reason to come back |
| Stardew Valley | its published look: warm saturated ramps, a 3/4 view, wooden UI frames, day and night with lamps, rain | warmth, the hour as a mood, lamps that matter at night, a UI that belongs to the world | a 2D tile map, so building is placing furniture, not shaping a world |
| Pixelated 3D games that render low and enlarge with nearest sampling | the technique, well known | crisp pixels on real geometry, and a fragment cost a phone can pay | none for us; it is the method |

Nothing is copied: no texture, sprite, sound, font or layout comes from these games. Every pixel is drawn from data in this repository (D4).

## Decision

### D1. A full-screen route with its own shell

The game lives at `/{locale}/island`, a route of its own beside `_main`, so no sidebar, header or page frame draws around it. Its gate sends a visitor to sign-in as the account screens do, it renders only in the browser (ADR 0038 D2), and it stays out of indexes. The canvas covers the screen (`100dvh`, safe areas respected); the game's HUD draws over it. Leaving returns to the page the learner came from, or home.

### D2. Engine: a WebGL2 renderer of our own

| Option | First chunk on a phone (gzip) | Pixel control | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| three.js with React Three Fiber (the first island) | about 170 KB plus the reconciler | through post passes | rejected: weight, and a scene graph we do not need |
| three.js, imperative | about 150 KB | good | rejected: weight for one chunk shader and one sprite shader |
| Babylon.js (the browser Minecraft) | over 1 MB | good | rejected |
| WebGL2, our own renderer | about 15 KB for the renderer, 40 KB for the game with its HUD | exact | **chosen** |

The renderer is a handful of programs: chunk faces (opaque, then water and glass), billboard sprites, sky, weather particles, and one blit. The world draws into a small framebuffer whose size is the screen divided by the art pixel, sampled with `NEAREST` and enlarged to the screen, so every art pixel stays a crisp square. The art pixel is a whole number of device pixels chosen so the small side of the framebuffer is about 240 to 360 art pixels; zoom moves the camera, never the art pixel.

Everything that can be computed without WebGL lives in pure `.ts` modules with tests at 100% coverage: blocks, terrain, the edit overlay, meshing, lighting, the ray cast, the camera, textures, the sky, input gestures. The `.tsx` renderer only uploads what they return and draws.

### D3. The world is blocks

- A cell holds one block id (`u8`, 0 is air). The world is a square footprint of side `size(level)` (D7) and 32 cells high, sea level at 8.
- The terrain is generated from a seed and the size: a rounded island of grass over dirt over stone, sand at the shore, a pond, trees and flowers, water around it. It is never stored.
- What the learner changed is stored as edits over the terrain (D6). The world a learner sees is the terrain with their edits applied, so growing the island adds new land around the old one without touching anything they built.
- The world is meshed in chunks of 16 by 16 columns. Only faces that touch air or a see-through block are emitted, each with four-corner ambient occlusion and its light. An edit remeshes its chunk and the neighbour it borders, which is under 2 ms on a phone.
- Light: the sun's direction and the hour set a sky light; lanterns and lamps spread block light by flood fill over 15 steps; both are baked per vertex, so night with lanterns costs nothing extra to draw.

### D4. Art pipeline: pixels as data

- One palette of named ramps, warm and saturated, four tones each (shadow, base, light, highlight). Night, dusk and rain are tints applied in the shader, never new colors.
- Each block face is a 16 by 16 tile written as 16 rows of characters, one character to a palette tone, plus a seeded scatter of neighbour tones so a wall of the same block never repeats visibly. The atlas is built in memory at start (about 2 ms) and uploaded once; the game requests no image file.
- Sprites (animals, Nina, particles) are frames of characters the same way. An animal on land is a billboard that faces the camera and stands at its feet's depth; Nina lies flat on the sea and turns along her path.
- The hotbar and the block book draw each block as a small three-face cube from the same tiles, on a 2D canvas.

### D5. Input: touch first

| Gesture | Mouse and keys | Touch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place the held block | click a face | tap a face |
| Remove a block | right-click, or click with the pickaxe held | tap with the pickaxe held, or long-press |
| Pan | drag with the left button on empty sky or with the middle button, WASD or arrows | one-finger drag |
| Zoom | wheel, `+` and `-` | pinch |
| Turn | right-drag, `Q` and `E` (45-degree steps that ease) | two-finger twist, or the turn buttons |
| Pick a slot | `1` to `9`, wheel with `Shift` | tap the slot |
| Undo and redo | `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z` | the HUD's buttons |

A press that moves under 8 CSS pixels and lifts within 350 ms is a tap; anything else is a drag. Pans and turns keep a short inertia. The block a press would change is outlined before the press lands on a pointer that hovers.

### D6. Persistence: edits over the terrain

- The island row keeps `lifetime`, `badges`, `learned` and `timeZone` from ADR 0042 and gains `world`: the learner's edits packed as 4-byte records `(x, y, z, block)` sorted by cell, one record per cell, at most 16,384 (64 KB), and `version`.
- The fields the withdrawn island used (`layout`, `harvested`, `watered`, `visibility`, `snapshot`) go. Their rows are learner records from the eight days the first island was live; the backend change clears them on production with the Convex CLI right before it deploys, as ADR 0046 does for try-out rows, and the Stars ledger (`islandAwards`) and `lifetime` are not touched.
- `island.mutations.build` takes a batch of at most 128 edits and the version it was made against. The server decodes, refuses the whole batch on the first edit that is outside the footprint, below the bedrock row, a block the learner has not unlocked, or made against an older version, then writes the merged edits and the next version. The client applies each edit at once, sends the batch after 600 ms of quiet or at 32 edits, and rolls back what the server refuses. Undo and redo send the inverse edits.
- Building is free: an unlocked block can be placed as often as the learner likes. Nothing is spent, so nothing needs a refund and no balance can drift from the ledger.

### D7. Progression: Stars unlock the world

Stars come only from learning (ADR 0042 D8 and D9, unchanged). The level table keeps its thresholds; each level now grows the footprint and unlocks a shelf of blocks and items.

| Level | Lifetime Stars | Footprint | Unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 | 16 by 16 | grass, dirt, sand, log, planks, leaves, flowers |
| 2 | 150 | 24 by 24 | stone, cobblestone, glass, water, fence |
| 3 | 400 | 32 by 32 | brick, red roof, white wall, path |
| 4 | 900 | 40 by 40 | lantern, blue roof, wool in eight colors |
| 5 | 1,800 | 48 by 48 | bookshelf, lamp post, garden bed |
| 6 | 3,500 | 56 by 56 | marble, gold, stained glass |
| 7 | 6,000 | 64 by 64 | everything else |

Badges (ADR 0042 D11) unlock one special item each. The HUD shows the level, the Stars to the next one, and a block book where every locked block says the level that opens it and how Stars are earned (+5 a lesson, +15 a try-out section, +40 a finished set). The table lives in one module that the server's build check and the HUD both read.

### D8. Life, time and weather

The hour follows the learner's zone: dawn, day, dusk and night change the sky, the sun's angle and the light (D3). Weather comes from the existing `/api/weather` route: clear, clouds, rain, storm and snow change the sky tint, the particles and the sound. Hens wander the grass, ducks float off the shore, and Nina swims her loop around the island. Clouds drift as their shadows, cut into whole art pixels in the chunk shader, so the top-down camera never looks through a cloud at the island. Sound is synthesized in Web Audio, starts after the first press and keeps one switch the browser remembers.

### D10. A finished village at one real scale

The owner's rules of 9 Nov: the game plays like a cozy life sim seen from above, not a first-person block game, and the world a learner opens is already finished and alive.

- One unit, written once in `lib/island/scale.ts` and read by every builder and sprite. A block is 0.9 m, a person is 2 blocks tall, a door is 2, a storey is 3, a cottage is 7 by 6 and 7 high with its chimney, a grown tree is 6 to 8 (about a cottage's height), a lamp post is 3, a hen or a duck half a block. `items.test.ts` measures every item against it, so no house is ever smaller than a tree.
- Every world opens on a village laid by `lib/island/village.ts` over the flat middle of the island: a square with a well, two paths crossing it, three cottages with their doors on a path, a fenced garden, lamp posts that light at night, and trees. A ring of open meadow around it is flat, so a learner has room to build.
- Villagers are animals two blocks tall (a cat, a rabbit and a bear), drawn at the blocks' own sixteen pixels a block as a head tile over a body tile, walking the paths. Hens and ducks are drawn at their real height.
- Ready-made items (a cottage, a tree, a well, a lamp post) sit in the block book above the blocks. A press places the whole item with its front to the camera, as one step undo takes back. It is refused when any part would leave the footprint, cut into a block, or stand over nothing, so nothing floats. An item opens at the latest level of its blocks, and the server checks each block as it does for single blocks.
- The footprint opens at 52 cells and grows to 64 with Stars.
- The sea runs to the horizon and is fogged per pixel, so the world never looks cut off. The HUD shows the learner's time of day beside the level.

Seasons follow the learner's own date and time zone, so every season the world has appears where it really falls: spring, summer, autumn and winter in temperate zones (half a year apart south of the tropics), and the wet and dry seasons in the tropics, where most Nakafa learners live. `lib/island/season.ts` repaints the atlas's grass and leaf tones for the season, so snow lies on the grass in winter, leaves turn in autumn and blossom in spring, and the grass dries in the dry season. The HUD shows the season's icon beside the clock.

Next, each in its own change: an unbounded world streamed in chunks with signed coordinates, a species catalog of more than a hundred animals and a hundred plants from parametric pixel families, the learner's own character, fishing and farming, and visiting another learner's world.

### D11. A living catalog of species

The owner's rule of 9 Nov: the world is alive with many kinds of animals and plants, at least a hundred of each.

- Every animal is a real species (174 today), kept in `lib/island/fauna` by kind (beasts, grazers, birds, waters, critters) with its family, coat, pattern, traits, real height in blocks and home. Its name is the dictionary's `animal-` key, in every language.
- Sprites are drawn from about twenty families of shapes (`fauna/families.ts`) by one painter (`tiles/shape.ts`) that colors each part, lights it from above, shades it below and outlines it in its own darkest tone, so a hundred and seventy species read as one tidy pixel style. A test keeps every drawing different from every other.
- Each day brings a few species of every home to the island, picked by the island's seed and the date: walkers on the meadow and the shore, flyers circling over the village, fish swimming off the shore, insects around the garden. Each is drawn at its real height, so a giraffe stands taller than a cottage and a beetle is a speck.
- The block book ends with a field guide of every species, its picture and its name.

Plants come next in the same way: a hundred species of flowers, crops, shrubs and trees from families of shapes, placeable as blocks.

### D12. The learner's own character

The owner's rule of 9 Nov: the learner has their own character who lives in the world, as in a cozy life sim, never a first-person camera.

- The learner is a fox, two blocks tall, drawn like the villagers. It starts on the village square.
- With their feet in hand (the first hotbar button) a press sends the fox along the shortest way to the pressed column (`lib/island/path.ts`): over columns it can stand on, with room for its body, climbing or dropping at most one block a step, never over water. W, A, S and D walk it a cell at a time as the camera faces; the arrow keys pan and Q and E turn the camera.
- The camera follows the walking fox.

### D13. The book, the cursor and the weather, shown rather than told

The owner's polish round of 9 Nov.

- The block book is a sheet: from the bottom on a phone, from the right on a wide screen. Icon tabs open its shelves (items, plants, blocks, animals), a search filters the open shelf, and only the shelf scrolls. The Stars each kind of learning earns show as icon chips.
- The cursor over the world shows the action: the held block itself while building, a pickaxe while digging, a hammer with a ready-made item, footprints while walking, and a grabbing hand while dragging. Every HUD button shows a pointer.
- Rain is faint and sparse, its streaks fading along their tails, so the world shows through it.
- The camera opens close on the village, so villagers, animals and the fox read at a glance.

### D14. The world settles

The owner's rule of 9 Nov: the physics is real. After every change, each touched column settles in the same step undo takes back (`lib/island/gravity.ts`). A plant whose ground is gone is lost, and sand falls until it rests, sinking through water to the floor. Built blocks (walls, roofs, planks, stone) hold where they are, as built things do. Ready-made items need solid ground under their whole base (D10), and the fox walks only where it can stand (D12).

### D15. Fishing

With the rod in hand, a press on water within nine cells of the fox casts a float there (`lib/island/fishing.ts`). A fish bites one and a half to four and a half seconds later, and the float dips hard. A tap during the bite (just over a second) reels in a real species of the sea, picked by the island's seed and the number of casts. A tap too early, or none in time, and it gets away. A catch shows its picture and name over the world and marks it in the field guide.

### D16. Farming

The hoe tills grass or dirt with open sky above it into farmland, clearing a wild plant there. A field crop placed on farmland (rice, corn, cabbage, tomato, chili, carrot, cassava, sweet potato) is sown as its sprout. The watering can wets the farmland. A sprout on wet farmland ripens into its crop after forty seconds of play, and the soil dries. The hoe on a ripe crop harvests it into the basket, which the HUD counts. Farmland, wet farmland and the eight sprouts are blocks (ids 140 to 149, `confect/island/farm.ts`), so a farm keeps across visits. Growth is written to the server but is no step undo takes back. On a phone the hotbar keeps three blocks beside its tools.

### D17. The learner's home grows

The village's north-west cottage is the learner's home (`lib/island/home.ts`). The block book's items open with it. At level 3 it grows into a two-storey house, and at level 4 into a manor with brick below, white walls above and a slate roof. Each tier stands on the same plot, so it never pushes into a neighbour. An upgrade builds every cell of the new tier and empties what the old one no longer needs, as one step undo takes back. It is refused when something the learner built stands where the new walls go. Houses are built from a style of storeys, walls and roof (`houseParts` in `items.ts`), so the ready-made cottage and the home share one builder.

### D20. Crisp pixels from every angle

The owner's rule of 13 Nov: the world is true 3D drawn in pixels, crisp and tidy at every angle and zoom, never blurry or ambiguous. The frame is still drawn small and enlarged by whole pixels (D2). Four things keep it crisp:

- Each tile has its own smaller levels (`lib/island/mips.ts`), averaged by opacity and never mixed with a neighbour's pixels, so a face far away or seen edge-on shows its own color instead of a shimmer of every pixel it holds.
- The solid pass also writes each face's direction, from how the world moves across the pixel, beside the colors. An edge pass then reads colors, directions and depth and draws a dark outline one art pixel wide on the near side of every silhouette, and a light line where a top meets a side. A depth step counts only past what the face's own slant explains, so a long slope seen edge-on stays clean. Nothing under the sea's surface is edged, so the sea floor's seams never show through the water.
- The camera's target slides across the screen's plane onto the grid of art pixels (`snapOrbit`). The enlarged frame moves back by what was rounded away, so a pan glides while the pixels hold still.
- Rain, snow and the aim outline are painted after the edge pass, against the scene's depth, so they are never edged.

The cost is one more full-frame pass over about 100,000 art pixels on a phone, with four neighbour reads each, and no change to any mesh.

### D21. True 3D bodies at real scale

The herd and the learner's fox are no longer flat sprites. Each is a low-poly model built in code from parts (`lib/island/models`):

- boxes, tapered boxes and lofts through cross-sections, turned, tipped and mirrored, with every face pointing outward under test
- the villagers and the fox at 1.8 m to the ear tips, two blocks, cozy, with heads a quarter of their height so a face reads
- a dairy cow, 1.4 m at the shoulder and 2.4 m long; hens and ducks at 45 cm
- a young humpback whale, 13 m long, on a slow round of the island in deep water, its back rising to break the sea

One program draws every body of a kind in a single instanced call. It places, turns and bobs each body from its own five numbers (`lib/island/bodies.ts`), lights its faces flat in the same few steps of light as the blocks, and writes their direction, so the edge pass outlines a cow as crisply as a house. Before the bodies, the same meshes pressed flat draw a soft shadow on the ground, which writes no depth and no edge, so nothing seems to float. The flat sprites of the herd, and their tiles, are gone. The opening camera frames 16 cells instead of 24, so a person reads at a glance. Wild visitors and the sea's swimmers became models with D24 and D25; plants follow, and Nina and the fishing float stay flat.

### D22. Nothing passes through anything

The owner's rule of 13 Nov: no clipping. Every body takes the room its own model measures (`lib/island/room.ts`): half its width around its middle, and for a long body such as a cow its front and back along the way it faces, up to its full height.

- A body fits where every point of that footprint stands on the floor under its middle, with nothing but air or plants above it. A walker never climbs onto a step or a wall.
- A step into a wall, a step or another body stops the walker, and it picks a new way. A step that moves it away from a body it already touches is allowed, so two bodies never lock together.
- A new game places each animal where it fits, apart from every animal placed before it.
- The learner's fox waits for an animal in its way instead of walking through it.
- The fox's own route already keeps to columns with room for a person (D12), and a ready-made item needs room for every cell (D10).

### D23. An archipelago, never one island alone

The owner's rule of 13 Nov: many islands, with lakes inside them. The learner's own island keeps its footprint and its edits. It gains a pond in its meadow beyond the village, toward a side its seed picks, at least three cells from the sea and one block under the ground, with a sand rim where ducks can swim.

Six isles ring it (`lib/island/isles.ts`), each its own size (6 to 15 cells across its radius), height and cover. Each grows from its seed as a heightfield: a beach at its edge, grass over it, stone on its tallest hills, a wood on half of them, and a lake in the middle of the large ones. Every isle's near shore lies at least 58 cells from the middle, past the whale's round, and no two touch.

The isles are generated, never stored, and nobody builds on them. They are drawn as one static mesh in the chunk program's layout, set once a game and drawn with the blocks in a single call. The sea's own surface covers their beaches' feet and their lakes. The camera may now pull back to 180 blocks to see the whole ring. The home page's tour leaves them out, to keep the home's first load light.

### D24. Life in the open sea

The owner's rule of 13 Nov: whales, sharks and more animals, never an empty sea. Six big swimmers share the sea with the humpback (`seaBodies` in `lib/island/bodies.ts`, models from `lib/island/models/ocean.ts`): a reef shark, a pod of three dolphins and a pair of orcas between the whale's round and the isles' nearest shore, and a hammerhead, a tiger shark and a whale shark beyond the isles' farthest. Each kind keeps a round of its own at its own depth and pace, from a start the island's seed picks, so no two rounds ever cross and nothing swims through an isle; a test measures every round against every body's width and the isles of several seeds. A dolphin leaps clear of the sea now and then; the rest keep under it and cast no shadow.

The day's sea visitors (D11) keep to water at least three blocks deep, four cells past the farthest the island's land can reach (`shoreOf` in `lib/island/terrain.ts`). A waterbird floats on the surface, a lobster walks the sea floor, a fish swims in a shoal of its kind (`schoolOf`, up to nine of the smallest), and an eel, a ray, a turtle or a sea snake swims alone. Each one under the surface stays between the floor and the surface; one with no water under it is left out rather than drawn through the ground.

### D25. Every animal a true 3D model

Every wild visitor is its species' own low-poly model at its real size (`speciesFaces`), drawn like the herd: one mesh a species, built the first time the island meets it, and every body of it in one instanced call. A body carries the height of the ground its shadow falls on: a walker's at its feet, a flyer's on the ground below it, and none over water or under it. Walkers stop at a step or a wall and rest instead of passing through. The species' flat tiles leave the GPU's atlas; the block book's field guide still paints them.

### D26. Phones stay fast

The owner's rule of 13 Nov: the island stays fast on a phone. Two loops ran past the D9 budget. The game redrew its water and its wandering life every 66 ms, or 33 ms in rain, on every device. That redraw now also waits for `createFramePace`, so a phone that takes 20 ms a frame draws the world moving by itself about six times a second, while a camera turn, a walk or a cast still draws on the next frame. The home's tour kept drawing after the reader scrolled past it. It now rests while its canvas is out of sight, and goes on from where it stopped when it comes back.

### D9. Performance budget

| Budget | Value |
| --- | --- |
| A route's first JavaScript | unchanged on every other route; the island route's own shell under the account screens' budget |
| Game chunk | under 60 KB gzip, loaded with `import()` after the route's shell paints |
| Framebuffer | the screen divided by the art pixel: about 100,000 pixels on a phone |
| Draw calls | under 40 for a 64 by 64 world |
| Frames | on demand while nothing moves; ambient life at 30 a second at most, kept to `createFramePace`; nothing while the page is hidden |
| Remesh | one chunk under 2 ms on a phone |

## Considered Designs

**Keep ADR 0042's orthographic island and enlarge it.** Rejected: a fixed 2:1 isometric camera cannot turn freely or come close, and the owner asked for a world the learner moves through.

**First person like Minecraft.** Rejected: pointer lock does not exist on a phone, and building a house from inside it on a small screen is slow. An orbiting camera over the island keeps every block in reach with one tap.

**Store the whole voxel grid.** Rejected: 131,072 bytes a learner for a 64 by 64 island, rewritten on every save, while a house is a few hundred edits over generated land.

**Spend Stars per block.** Rejected: the owner asked that Stars unlock blocks; spending would bring back balances, refunds and the farming pressure D15 of the plan forbids.

## Consequences

- A learner's Stars, earned since 8 November, open the island at the level they reached; only the withdrawn island's placements are cleared.
- The game's code never reaches another route, and a phone that never enters never downloads it.
- New blocks and items are a few lines of data: a tile, a row in the block table, a level.
- The renderer is ours to maintain: about a thousand lines of WebGL and pure modules, against a dependency of over half a megabyte.
