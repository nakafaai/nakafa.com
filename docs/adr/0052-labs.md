# ADR 0052: A Lab Composes The Engine

## Status

Accepted on 27 November 2026, on the owner's rule of that day: "building blocks scalable, 100% consistent, so we have consistent high quality no matter which AI model builds the 3D later; sharp, realistic, no slop, 0 cartoonish." This page is what a model reads before it builds or changes a 3D lab. `scripts/check/labs.ts` enforces it.

## Context

On main at 15:37 on 27 November, 178 lab folders under `packages/design-system/components/contents` held about 2,660 bare geometries and 2,080 bare materials. A lab that draws its own spheres and boxes looks as good as the model that wrote it, which is why labs read as toys. The engine under `packages/design-system/components/three` already names real things (brass, a beaker, a nucleus, a micrometer) and gives them their realism. Labs that compose it look the same, whoever writes them.

## Decision

### D1. A lab composes engine parts only

A module under `packages/design-system/components/contents` (tests aside) never:

- writes a three.js geometry or material element: `<boxGeometry>`, `<meshStandardMaterial>`, `<shaderMaterial>`, any intrinsic ending in `Geometry` or `Material`;
- builds one: `new BoxGeometry(...)`, `new THREE.MeshPhysicalMaterial(...)`, any class ending in `Geometry` or `Material`, and any `new ...Loader(...)`;
- loads a model or a raster texture (`useGLTF`, `useTexture`, `useLoader` and their kin), or places a raw object with `<primitive>`;
- imports a drei part that draws a bare shape or line (`RoundedBox`, `Sphere`, `Line` and their kin) or brings its own material (`PointMaterial`, `MeshTransmissionMaterial`).

`<group>` places and moves parts. `<mesh>`, `<points>` and `<line>` stay, because the engine's shapes and materials attach inside them (`<mesh><Turn profile={BEAKER} /><Matter of="glass" /></mesh>`); with every bare part refused, they hold nothing else.

`scripts/check/pending.txt` lists the folders that still fail, one per line, sorted. A listed folder is skipped, a folder not listed must pass, and a listed folder that passes fails the check until its line is removed. Delete your folder's line in the change that converts it. When the last line goes, the file and the pending list in `scripts/check/pending.ts` go with it.

### D2. The order of work

1. Place. Wrap the lab in `<ScienceStage setting="...">` from `three/stage` and name where it stands: `lab`, `field`, `sky`, `water`, `studio`, `space`, `dark`, `cell` or `microscope`. Give `floor` its height when things stand on it, and `ground` when it is not the place's own. The stage owns lights, environment, shadows, fog, ground, sky, haze and post-processing. Never add them in a lab.
2. Subject. Build the subject from engine parts: a named kit first (`three/instrument`, `three/organism`, `three/molecule`, `three/atom`, `three/vessel`), else `three/form` shapes dressed in `three/matter`. Real proportions, in real units.
3. Camera. `<CameraControls framing="content" />` from `three/camera`, with the subject inside `<CameraBounds bounds={...}>` and decoration inside `<CameraBounds exclude>`. The subject fills at least 80% of the binding side of the frame at 390 px and at 1280 px.
4. Teaching layer. Labels, readings, vectors and graphs are overlays from the engine (`three/overlay`, `three/label`, `three/arrow`, `three/print`), never scenery. When values do not fit beside their arrows, they go to the card's footer readout in matching colours.
5. Motion. Drive motion in `useFrame` through refs, with no allocation per frame. A paused, static or reduced-motion lab renders on demand.

A new engine folder gets its row here in the change that lands it. `scripts/check/catalogue.ts` fails while a folder under `packages/design-system/components/three` is missing from this table, or while the table names a module that does not exist.

### D4. A missing part goes into the engine

When no module draws what the lab needs, add it to the engine as a named part with a small interface: the author names what it is (a 250 mL beaker, a hose from A to B, an epoxy bench) and gets the realism. Logic lives in a `.ts` module with its test at 100%; the React wrapper in `.tsx` stays thin. The module owns its geometry and textures, shares them, follows the stage's tier for segment counts and texture sizes, and disposes of them. Never work round a missing part inside the lab.

### D5. Physically true

1. Nothing passes through anything. Water never shows inside a hull, a glass or a tank wall. Nothing sinks into ground, bank, table or another object at the start, at the end, or at the extremes of any control.
2. Nothing floats. What stands on the ground touches it, with a contact shadow. A road, a path or a floor is part of the terrain, never a raised slab with visible sides.
3. No edge of the world. Ground, water, ice and sky run to the horizon and melt into haze. No rim of a disc or plane, no hard horizon band, no dark corner, no blurred stripe.
4. Weather and effects are physical. Rain is thin, fast, semi-transparent streaks with splashes and a wet sheen. Smoke, steam, spray, foam and dust have volume and soft edges.
5. Blur never smears the ground, the horizon or the subject.
6. Vectors, graphs and labels are a clean teaching layer. Arrows start on their object, are thick and long enough to read at 390 px, and sit against a calm background. Labels never touch each other or the arrows. A reader understands the overlay in two seconds.
7. Light and dark are both looked at. A fault in one of them is a fault.

### D6. Labels

Every label reads in two seconds at 390 and 1280 px, in light and in dark.

1. A label sits next to the part it names, with a short leader when it needs one. Nothing floats in empty sky or in a corner.
2. No label overlaps another label or its subject.
3. Math renders once: a formula is drawn as math or as text, never both.
4. No label is smaller than 14 CSS px. Never cap `maximumFontSize` below 14.

### D7. The evidence a pull request shows

Render every changed lab in a real browser, before and after, light and dark, at 1280 and 390 px wide, with zero console and page errors. A reviewer blocks the merge on any one of:

1. Anything passing through anything.
2. Anything floating.
3. A visible edge of the world.
4. Unphysical weather or effects.
5. Blur that smears ground, horizon or subject.
6. An overlay or a label that is not readable in two seconds, or that breaks D6.
7. A subject under 80% of the frame, or an after that is less alive or less readable than its before.
8. A lab folder that still fails `bun run check:tests`, or a pending line left for a folder that passes.

### D8. The frame budget

60 frames a second on a phone, never bought by lowering quality. Speed comes from shared geometry and textures (the engine caches them), instancing for repeated parts, the demand frame loop for anything that is not moving, and zero allocation per frame: reuse vectors, colours and matrices held in refs. Segment counts and texture sizes follow the stage's tier, never a number written in a lab.

## Consequences

- A lab reads the same in every hand: the engine decides how brass, glass, skin and ground look, and a lab only says what is there.
- The engine grows by named parts with tests, so a part made for one lab serves the next.
- The pending list only shrinks, and parallel conversions each delete their own line and merge without conflict.
